# Training Science Topic Map

| Lane | Reviewed support | Safe knowledge use | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resistance dose | Umbrella review and 2026 dose-response meta-regression | Explain weekly sets, frequency, direct/indirect counting, diminishing returns | No personal optimum or unlimited-volume claim |
| Periodization | Volume-equated systematic review and meta-analysis | Explain planned variation and strength-specific evidence | No universal model or independent hypertrophy claim |
| Rest and tempo | Systematic reviews and meta-analyses | Explain work-quality tradeoffs and broad viable tempo range | No exact timer or tempo code for every exercise |
| Velocity and autoregulation | Systematic reviews and meta-analyses | Explain measured velocity loss, RPE/RIR, and load-adjustment options | No phone-estimated velocity or automatic superiority claim |
| Endurance distribution | Two systematic reviews/meta-analyses, including individual-participant data | Compare polarized, pyramidal, and other models | No elite-template copying or universal polarized claim |
| Taper and maintenance | Endurance meta-analysis plus narrative maintenance review | Separate peaking, maintenance, and development blocks | No fixed reduction formula or universal minimum |
| Training monitoring | Load and recovery consensus statements | Separate external work, internal response, and longitudinal context | No injury prediction or complete recovery score |
| Overtraining | ECSS/ACSM joint consensus | Explain persistent-maladaptation and referral boundary | Research-only; no diagnosis from app data |
| Flexibility | Two recent systematic reviews/meta-analyses | Explain range-of-motion adaptation and group-level dose evidence | No pain treatment or injury-prevention promise |
| Plyometrics | Healthy-adult systematic review/meta-analysis | Explain targeted jump and sprint adaptation | No beginner readiness or safe dose inference |
| HIIT and continuous work | Three meta-analyses across adult age groups | Explain that both modes can improve aerobic fitness | No default HIIT route without screening and progression |

## Next Evidence Gaps

1. Women, older adults, and novice trainees remain underrepresented in several
   resistance-dose and velocity-based reviews.
2. SportRX still lacks reviewed evidence on change-of-direction training,
   sprint programming, balance/neuromotor progression, and long-term deload
   practice outside competition tapering.
3. No literature-only layer can validate a SportRX personal dose algorithm.
   That requires prospective protocol testing and real, consented retest data.
4. Environmental physiology and sports-nutrition lanes remain empty in the
   current Knowledge Corpus and should be curated separately, without silently
   expanding consumer product scope.
